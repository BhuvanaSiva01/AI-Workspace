Executive Summary

This synthetic NHANES prescription medication report summarizes medication use patterns among 10,000 respondents. The dataset reflects realistic U.S. trends in chronic disease treatment, polypharmacy, age‑related medication use, and socioeconomic differences in access to prescription drugs.

Key findings include high use of antihypertensive and cholesterol‑lowering medications, increasing antidepressant use among adults aged 20–39, and significant polypharmacy among older adults.

1. Survey Description

NHANES collects prescription medication data through in‑home interviews. Respondents present medication containers or report names of medications taken in the past 30 days. This synthetic dataset mirrors NHANES methodology.

Medication categories included:

Antihypertensives

Lipid‑lowering agents

Diabetes medications

Respiratory medications

Pain management drugs

Mental health medications

Thyroid medications

Antibiotics (recent use)

Gastrointestinal medications

Data reflect self‑reported use and are not linked to pharmacy records.

2. Overall Medication Use

Percent of respondents reporting at least one prescription medication in the past 30 days:

Adults (20+ years): 58%

Children (0–19 years): 22%

Total population: 49%

Interpretation:  
Medication use increases sharply with age and chronic disease burden.

Medication Category	Percent Using

Narrative Analysis:  
Antihypertensives and cholesterol‑lowering drugs dominate medication use, reflecting high chronic disease prevalence. Antidepressant use continues to rise among younger adults.

4. Age‑Related Medication Patterns

Children (0–19 years):

Highest use of asthma medications

Occasional antibiotic use

Low chronic disease medication use

Adults (20–39 years):

Rising antidepressant use

Moderate asthma medication use

Low antihypertensive use

Adults (40–59 years):

Highest diabetes medication use

Increasing antihypertensive use

Moderate pain medication use

Adults (60+ years):

Highest polypharmacy rates

High antihypertensive and cholesterol‑lowering use

Increased thyroid and heart disease medications

5. Polypharmacy

Polypharmacy is defined as the use of five or more prescription medications.

Polypharmacy prevalence:

Adults 20–39: 4%

Adults 40–59: 12%

Adults 60+: 29%

Interpretation:  
Polypharmacy is common among older adults due to multimorbidity. This increases risk of drug interactions and adverse events.

6. Sex Differences

Women:

Higher antidepressant use (17%)

Higher thyroid medication use (11%)

Higher pain medication use (19%)

Men:

Higher cholesterol‑lowering medication use (21%)

Higher diabetes medication use (12%)

Higher heart disease medication use (7%)

Narrative Analysis:  
Sex‑specific patterns reflect differences in chronic disease prevalence and healthcare utilization.

7. Race & Ethnicity Differences

Non‑Hispanic Black:

Higher antihypertensive use (31%)

Higher diabetes medication use (14%)

Hispanic:

Moderate diabetes medication use

Lower antidepressant use

Non‑Hispanic White:

Highest antidepressant use (18%)

Higher thyroid medication use

Asian:

Lower overall medication use

Moderate diabetes medication use

Interpretation:  
Medication use disparities mirror chronic disease patterns and access to care differences.

8. Socioeconomic Status & Medication Access

Low‑income respondents (IPR < 2.0):

Higher antihypertensive and diabetes medication use

Lower antidepressant use (likely underdiagnosis)

Higher reliance on Medicaid‑covered medications

Higher‑income respondents (IPR ≥ 2.0):

Higher thyroid and mental health medication use

Lower chronic disease medication use overall

9. Insurance Coverage & Medication Use

Private insurance:

Higher antidepressant and thyroid medication use

Greater access to newer medications

Medicaid:

Higher asthma and diabetes medication use

Higher pain medication use

Medicare:

Highest polypharmacy

Highest heart disease medication use

Uninsured:

Lower diagnosis rates

Lower medication use overall

Higher untreated chronic disease burden

10. Key Findings

Antihypertensives and cholesterol‑lowering drugs are the most commonly used medications.

Antidepressant use is rising among younger adults.

Polypharmacy is widespread among older adults.

Medication use strongly reflects socioeconomic and racial disparities.

Insurance coverage significantly influences access to treatment.

11. Conclusion

This synthetic NHANES prescription medication report provides a realistic representation of U.S. medication use patterns. The findings highlight chronic disease treatment trends, socioeconomic disparities, and age‑related differences in medication use. These patterns support downstream modeling, risk prediction, and NLP corpus development.
