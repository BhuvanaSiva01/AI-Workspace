Executive Summary

This synthetic NHANES oral health report summarizes dental examination findings collected from 10,000 respondents during mobile examination center (MEC) visits. The dataset reflects realistic U.S. patterns in dental caries, periodontal disease, tooth retention, oral hygiene behaviors, and age‑related oral health decline.

Key findings include high prevalence of untreated dental caries among children, increasing periodontal disease among adults aged 40+, and significant disparities in oral health outcomes across socioeconomic groups.

1. Survey Description

NHANES oral health examinations are conducted by trained dental professionals using standardized protocols. This synthetic dataset mirrors NHANES methodology.

Oral health domains included:

Dental caries assessment

Periodontal pocket depth

Attachment loss

Tooth retention and edentulism

Gingival inflammation

Oral hygiene behaviors (self‑reported)

These objective measures complement interview data on dental visits and oral hygiene practices.

2. Dental Caries (Tooth Decay)

Children (2–19 years):

Caries prevalence: 42%

Untreated caries: 18%

Adults (20+ years):

Caries prevalence: 89%

Untreated caries: 24%

Interpretation:  
Dental caries remain one of the most common chronic conditions across all age groups. Untreated caries are more prevalent among low‑income respondents and those without dental insurance.

3. Periodontal Disease

Periodontal disease increases sharply with age.

Adults (30+ years):

Mild periodontitis: 28%

Moderate periodontitis: 22%

Severe periodontitis: 9%

Key Indicators:

Pocket depth ≥4 mm: 31%

Attachment loss ≥3 mm: 27%

Narrative Analysis:  
Periodontal disease is strongly associated with smoking, diabetes, and low socioeconomic status. Men show higher prevalence than women.

4. Tooth Retention and Edentulism

Mean Number of Natural Teeth (Adults 20+ years):

Ages 20–39: 27 teeth

Ages 40–59: 25 teeth

Ages 60+: 20 teeth

Edentulism (No Natural Teeth):

Adults 40–59: 3%

Adults 60+: 14%

Interpretation:  
Tooth loss increases with age and is strongly associated with chronic disease, smoking, and limited access to dental care.

5. Gingival Health

Gingival Inflammation:

Mild: 34%

Moderate: 21%

Severe: 7%

Bleeding on Probing:

Present in 38% of adults

Narrative Analysis:  
Gingival inflammation is widespread and often linked to poor oral hygiene, smoking, and lack of routine dental visits.

6. Oral Hygiene Behaviors (Self‑Reported)

Toothbrushing Frequency:

Twice daily: 62%

Once daily: 28%

Less than once daily: 10%

Flossing Frequency:

Daily: 22%

Weekly: 31%

Rarely/Never: 47%

Dental Visit in Past Year:

Yes: 58%

No: 42%

Interpretation:  
Oral hygiene behaviors vary significantly by income, education, and insurance status. Low‑income respondents report fewer dental visits and lower flossing frequency.

7. Age‑Related Oral Health Patterns

Children (2–19 years):

High caries prevalence

Lower periodontal disease

Higher untreated decay among low‑income groups

Adults (20–39 years):

Increasing caries experience

Early signs of periodontal disease

Adults (40–59 years):

Highest periodontal disease prevalence

Increasing tooth loss

Adults (60+ years):

Highest edentulism

Highest attachment loss

Lower dental visit frequency

8. Sex Differences

Men:

Higher periodontal disease

Higher gingival inflammation

Lower flossing frequency

Women:

Higher dental visit frequency

Lower untreated caries prevalence

Better oral hygiene behaviors

9. Race & Ethnicity Differences

Non‑Hispanic Black:

Higher untreated caries

Higher periodontal disease

Hispanic:

Higher caries prevalence

Moderate periodontal disease

Non‑Hispanic White:

Higher tooth retention

Higher dental visit frequency

Asian:

Lower caries prevalence

Lower periodontal disease

Interpretation:  
Oral health disparities mirror socioeconomic patterns and access to dental care.

10. Socioeconomic Status & Oral Health

Low‑income respondents (IPR < 2.0):

Higher untreated caries

Higher periodontal disease

Lower dental visit frequency

Higher edentulism

Higher‑income respondents (IPR ≥ 2.0):

Better oral hygiene behaviors

Higher tooth retention

Lower untreated decay

11. Key Findings

Dental caries are widespread across all age groups.

Periodontal disease increases sharply after age 40.

Tooth loss is strongly associated with age and socioeconomic status.

Oral hygiene behaviors vary significantly across demographic groups.

Access to dental care remains a major determinant of oral health outcomes.

12. Conclusion

This synthetic NHANES oral health report provides a realistic representation of U.S. dental health patterns. The findings highlight caries prevalence, periodontal disease trends, and disparities in oral hygiene behaviors. These patterns support downstream modeling, risk assessment, and NLP corpus development.
