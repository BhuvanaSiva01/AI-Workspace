Executive Summary

This synthetic NHANES physical activity report summarizes movement patterns, exercise habits, and sedentary behavior among 10,000 respondents. The dataset reflects realistic U.S. trends in moderate‑to‑vigorous physical activity (MVPA), walking frequency, occupational activity, screen time, and age‑related declines in activity levels.

Key findings include low MVPA among adults aged 40+, high sedentary time among adolescents, and strong associations between physical activity and socioeconomic status.

1. Survey Description

NHANES collects physical activity data through structured questionnaires and accelerometer measurements (synthetic values in this dataset). Domains included:

Moderate‑to‑vigorous physical activity (MVPA)

Walking frequency

Occupational activity

Recreational activity

Sedentary time

Screen time

Muscle‑strengthening activities

These measures provide insight into lifestyle patterns that influence chronic disease risk.

2. Moderate‑to‑Vigorous Physical Activity (MVPA)

Adults (20+ years):

Mean MVPA: 112 minutes/week

Meeting guidelines (150+ min/week): 38%

Below guidelines: 62%

Children & Adolescents (6–19 years):

Mean MVPA: 68 minutes/day

Meeting guidelines (60+ min/day): 54%

Interpretation:  
Adults fall short of recommended activity levels, while children meet guidelines more frequently but show high variability.

3. Walking Frequency

Walking is the most common form of physical activity.

Adults (20+ years):

Walks ≥3 times/week: 46%

Walks <1 time/week: 22%

Narrative Analysis:  
Walking frequency declines with age and is strongly associated with neighborhood walkability and income.

4. Occupational Activity

Occupational activity varies widely by job type.

Sedentary occupations:

41% of adults

Mean sitting time: 7.4 hours/day

Moderate‑activity occupations:

37% of adults

High‑activity occupations:

22% of adults

Interpretation:  
Sedentary work contributes significantly to total daily sitting time, especially among higher‑income respondents.

5. Recreational Activity

Common recreational activities include:

Walking

Running

Cycling

Gym workouts

Sports

Swimming

Participation Rates:

Any recreational activity: 52%

Structured exercise (gym/sports): 29%

Narrative Analysis:  
Recreational activity participation is highest among adults aged 20–39 and lowest among adults aged 60+.

6. Muscle‑Strengthening Activities

Adults (20+ years):

Meets guidelines (≥2 days/week): 27%

Below guidelines: 73%

Interpretation:  
Strength training is less common than aerobic activity, despite its importance for metabolic health and aging.

7. Sedentary Behavior

Sedentary time includes sitting at work, screen time, transportation, and leisure.

Adults (20+ years):

Mean sedentary time: 8.1 hours/day

High sedentary time (≥10 hours/day): 19%

Children & Adolescents (6–19 years):

Mean sedentary time: 7.3 hours/day

Screen time ≥4 hours/day: 46%

Narrative Analysis:  
Sedentary behavior is highest among adolescents and adults with sedentary occupations.

8. Screen Time

Screen time includes TV, computers, tablets, and phones.

Adults (20+ years):

Mean screen time: 3.2 hours/day

Children & Adolescents (6–19 years):

Mean screen time: 4.8 hours/day

Interpretation:  
High screen time among adolescents is associated with lower MVPA and higher BMI.

9. Age‑Related Activity Patterns

Children (6–19 years):

Highest MVPA

Highest screen time

Moderate sedentary time

Adults (20–39 years):

Highest recreational activity

Moderate sedentary time

Adults (40–59 years):

Declining MVPA

Increasing sedentary time

Adults (60+ years):

Lowest MVPA

Highest sedentary time

Lowest strength training participation

10. Sex Differences

Men:

Higher MVPA

Higher strength training

Higher recreational activity

Women:

Higher walking frequency

Lower sedentary time

Lower screen time

11. Socioeconomic Status & Activity Levels

Low‑income respondents (IPR < 2.0):

Higher occupational activity

Lower recreational activity

Higher sedentary time outside work

Higher‑income respondents (IPR ≥ 2.0):

Higher recreational activity

Higher gym participation

Lower occupational activity

Interpretation:  
Socioeconomic status influences both activity type and total sedentary time.

12. Key Findings

Most adults do not meet physical activity guidelines.

Adolescents show high MVPA but also high screen time.

Sedentary occupations contribute heavily to daily sitting time.

Strength training participation is low across all age groups.

Socioeconomic status strongly influences activity patterns.

13. Conclusion

This synthetic NHANES physical activity report provides a realistic representation of U.S. movement patterns. The findings highlight low MVPA among adults, high sedentary time among adolescents, and strong socioeconomic disparities. These patterns support downstream modeling, risk assessment, and NLP corpus development.
