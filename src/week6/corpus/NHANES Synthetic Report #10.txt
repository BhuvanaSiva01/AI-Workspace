Executive Summary

This synthetic NHANES environmental exposure report summarizes biomarkers of chemical, metal, and pollutant exposure among 10,000 respondents. The dataset reflects realistic U.S. patterns in lead, mercury, cadmium, BPA, phthalates, pesticides, tobacco‑related biomarkers, and air‑quality‑related exposures.

Key findings include detectable levels of multiple environmental chemicals, higher exposure among smokers and low‑income respondents, and age‑related differences in metal concentrations.

1. Survey Description

NHANES environmental exposure testing provides objective measures of chemical and pollutant burden. This synthetic dataset mirrors NHANES methodology.

Biomarker domains included:

Heavy metals (lead, mercury, cadmium)

Industrial chemicals (BPA, phthalates)

Pesticides

Tobacco exposure (cotinine)

Air pollution indicators

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs)

Values are synthetic but modeled on realistic population distributions.

2. Heavy Metals

Blood Lead (µg/dL):

Mean: 1.2

Elevated (≥5): 2%

Blood Mercury (µg/L):

Mean: 1.1

Elevated (≥5): 1%

Blood Cadmium (µg/L):

Mean: 0.45

Elevated (≥1): 6%

Interpretation:  
Lead and mercury levels are generally low but elevated among older adults and respondents with occupational exposure. Cadmium is strongly associated with smoking.

3. Industrial Chemicals

Urinary BPA (µg/L):

Mean: 2.4

High exposure (≥5): 12%

Urinary Phthalates (µg/L):

Mean: 28

High exposure (≥50): 9%

Narrative Analysis:  
BPA and phthalate exposure is widespread due to plastics, food packaging, and consumer products. Higher levels are observed among children and low‑income respondents.

4. Pesticide Exposure

Urinary Organophosphate Metabolites (µg/L):

Mean: 3.1

Elevated (≥10): 4%

Pyrethroid Metabolites (µg/L):

Mean: 1.8

Elevated (≥5): 3%

Interpretation:  
Pesticide exposure is highest among agricultural workers and respondents living in rural areas.

5. Tobacco Exposure (Cotinine)

Cotinine is a biomarker of nicotine exposure.

Urinary Cotinine (ng/mL):

Mean: 35

Elevated (≥100): 12%

Breakdown:

Active smokers: Mean 210

Secondhand exposure: Mean 45

Non‑exposed: Mean 8

Narrative Analysis:  
Cotinine levels reflect smoking prevalence and secondhand smoke exposure, especially among low‑income households.

6. Air Pollution Indicators

Blood CRP (mg/L) — inflammation marker:

Mean: 2.4

Elevated (≥3): 28%

Urinary PAHs (polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons):

Mean: 0.9 µg/L

Elevated (≥2): 7%

Interpretation:  
Air pollution exposure correlates with urban residence, smoking, and occupational factors.

7. Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)

Urinary VOC Metabolites (µg/L):

Mean: 1.7

Elevated (≥4): 5%

Common sources include:

Paints

Cleaning products

Industrial emissions

Vehicle exhaust

Narrative Analysis:  
VOCs are higher among respondents living near major roadways or industrial areas.

8. Age‑Related Exposure Patterns

Children (6–11 years):

Higher BPA and phthalate levels

Lower heavy metal levels

Adolescents (12–19 years):

Higher cotinine variability

Moderate pesticide exposure

Adults (20–39 years):

Higher VOC exposure

Moderate heavy metal levels

Adults (40–59 years):

Rising cadmium levels (smoking association)

Moderate pesticide exposure

Adults (60+ years):

Highest lead and mercury levels

Lower BPA and phthalate levels

9. Sex Differences

Men:

Higher heavy metal levels

Higher VOC exposure

Higher cotinine levels

Women:

Higher BPA and phthalate levels

Higher pesticide metabolite levels

Lower heavy metal exposure

10. Socioeconomic Status & Environmental Exposure

Low‑income respondents (IPR < 2.0):

Higher BPA and phthalate exposure

Higher cotinine levels

Higher VOC exposure

Higher pesticide exposure

Higher‑income respondents (IPR ≥ 2.0):

Lower tobacco exposure

Lower VOC exposure

Higher mercury levels (fish consumption)

Interpretation:  
Environmental exposure disparities reflect housing conditions, occupation, consumer product use, and lifestyle factors.

11. Key Findings

Heavy metal exposure is generally low but elevated among older adults.

BPA and phthalate exposure is widespread across all age groups.

Tobacco exposure remains a major environmental risk factor.

Air pollution indicators show elevated inflammation among urban residents.

Socioeconomic disparities strongly influence exposure patterns.

12. Conclusion

This synthetic NHANES environmental exposure report provides a realistic representation of U.S. chemical and pollutant burden. The findings highlight heavy metal trends, industrial chemical exposure, tobacco‑related biomarkers, and socioeconomic disparities. These patterns support downstream modeling, risk assessment, and NLP corpus development.
