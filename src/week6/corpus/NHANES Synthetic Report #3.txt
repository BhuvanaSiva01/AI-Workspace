Executive Summary

This synthetic NHANES chronic disease report summarizes self‑reported medical conditions among 10,000 respondents. The dataset reflects realistic U.S. patterns in chronic disease prevalence, comorbidities, and demographic disparities. Conditions covered include diabetes, hypertension, asthma, arthritis, cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and respiratory disorders.

Key findings show high prevalence of hypertension and arthritis, increasing diabetes rates among adults aged 40–59, and strong associations between chronic disease burden and socioeconomic status.

1. Survey Description

NHANES collects medical condition data through structured household interviews. Respondents report whether a healthcare professional has diagnosed them with specific chronic conditions. This synthetic dataset mirrors NHANES methodology.

Included conditions:

Diabetes

Prediabetes

Hypertension

High cholesterol

Asthma

COPD

Arthritis

Heart disease

Stroke

Chronic kidney disease

Thyroid disorders

Data are self‑reported and not based on clinical measurements.

Interpretation:  
Hypertension remains the most common chronic condition. Arthritis prevalence is high among adults aged 50+. Diabetes and prediabetes continue to rise, reflecting national trends.

3. Age‑Related Patterns

Adults aged 40–59 show the highest burden of chronic disease.

Ages 20–39:

Low rates of heart disease and stroke

Moderate asthma prevalence

Rising obesity‑related conditions

Ages 40–59:

Highest diabetes and prediabetes rates

Significant hypertension prevalence

Increasing arthritis and chronic pain

Ages 60+:

Highest heart disease and stroke prevalence

High arthritis and mobility limitations

Increased COPD and chronic kidney disease

4. Sex Differences

Men:

Higher rates of hypertension (33%)

Higher diabetes prevalence (12%)

Higher heart disease (7%)

Women:

Higher arthritis prevalence (25%)

Higher asthma prevalence (11%)

Higher thyroid disorder prevalence (9%)

Interpretation:  
Sex‑specific patterns reflect biological and behavioral differences observed in national health surveillance.

5. Race & Ethnicity Differences

Non‑Hispanic Black:

Highest hypertension prevalence (41%)

Higher diabetes prevalence (14%)

Hispanic:

Higher prediabetes prevalence (22%)

Moderate asthma prevalence

Non‑Hispanic White:

Highest arthritis prevalence (26%)

Higher COPD prevalence (5%)

Asian:

Lower obesity‑related conditions

Moderate diabetes prevalence (10%)

Narrative Analysis:  
Chronic disease disparities align with socioeconomic patterns, access to care, and long‑term environmental exposures.

6. Comorbidity Patterns

Common comorbidity clusters:

Hypertension + high cholesterol (19%)

Diabetes + hypertension (8%)

Arthritis + chronic pain (14%)

Heart disease + chronic kidney disease (3%)

Interpretation:  
Comorbidities increase healthcare utilization and complicate disease management, especially among older adults.

7. Socioeconomic Status & Chronic Disease

Low‑income respondents (IPR < 2.0):

Higher hypertension (35%)

Higher diabetes (13%)

Higher asthma (12%)

Higher COPD (6%)

Higher‑income respondents (IPR ≥ 2.0):

Lower chronic disease burden overall

Higher rates of thyroid disorders (likely due to better diagnosis)

Narrative Analysis:  
Socioeconomic status remains one of the strongest predictors of chronic disease burden.

8. Insurance Coverage & Diagnosis Rates

Uninsured respondents show:

Lower diagnosis rates for early‑stage conditions

Higher prevalence of advanced disease

Lower treatment adherence

Medicaid respondents show:

Higher asthma and diabetes prevalence

Higher chronic pain and arthritis

Medicare respondents show:

Highest heart disease and stroke prevalence

Highest multimorbidity rates

9. Key Findings

Hypertension and arthritis are the most common chronic conditions.

Diabetes and prediabetes continue to rise among middle‑aged adults.

Chronic disease burden is strongly linked to socioeconomic status.

Minority groups show higher rates of hypertension and diabetes.

Comorbidities increase sharply after age 50.

10. Conclusion

This synthetic NHANES chronic disease report provides a realistic representation of U.S. medical condition patterns. The findings highlight persistent disparities, age‑related disease progression, and strong associations between socioeconomic status and chronic disease burden. These patterns support downstream modeling, risk prediction, and NLP corpus development.
