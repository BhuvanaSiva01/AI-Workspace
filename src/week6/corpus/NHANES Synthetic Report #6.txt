Executive Summary

This synthetic NHANES laboratory biomarker report summarizes blood and urine test results collected from 10,000 respondents during mobile examination center (MEC) visits. The dataset reflects realistic U.S. patterns in metabolic health, cardiovascular risk, liver and kidney function, hematology, and environmental exposures.

Key findings include elevated cholesterol levels among middle‑aged adults, rising HbA1c values associated with diabetes risk, and detectable levels of environmental chemicals in a subset of respondents.

1. Survey Description

NHANES laboratory testing provides objective health indicators that complement interview and physical examination data. This synthetic dataset mirrors NHANES methodology.

Laboratory domains included:

Metabolic biomarkers

Cardiovascular biomarkers

Liver function tests

Kidney function tests

Hematology

Nutritional biomarkers

Environmental exposure markers

All values are synthetic but modeled on realistic population distributions.

9. Age‑Related Laboratory Patterns

Children (0–19 years):

Lower cholesterol

Lower creatinine

Higher vitamin D variability

Adults (20–39 years):

Rising triglycerides

Moderate fasting glucose

Adults (40–59 years):

Highest LDL cholesterol

Highest fasting glucose

Increasing liver enzyme abnormalities

Adults (60+ years):

Highest creatinine

Highest eGFR reduction

Highest anemia prevalence

10. Key Findings

Prediabetes is widespread across adults.

Cholesterol abnormalities affect nearly one‑third of respondents.

Liver enzyme elevations correlate with obesity and alcohol intake.

Kidney function declines significantly after age 60.

Vitamin D deficiency is common across all demographic groups.

Environmental exposures are detectable but generally low.
