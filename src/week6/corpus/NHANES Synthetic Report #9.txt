Executive Summary

This synthetic NHANES mental health report summarizes depression screening results, psychological distress indicators, and mental health service utilization among 10,000 respondents. The dataset reflects realistic U.S. patterns in depressive symptoms, anxiety indicators, stress levels, and demographic disparities in mental health outcomes.

Key findings include elevated depressive symptoms among adolescents and adults aged 20–39, higher psychological distress among low‑income respondents, and significant gaps in access to mental health services.

1. Survey Description

NHANES collects mental health data through standardized questionnaires. This synthetic dataset mirrors NHANES methodology.

Domains included:

Depression screening (PHQ‑9 synthetic values)

Anxiety indicators

Psychological distress (K6 synthetic values)

Sleep quality

Stress levels

Mental health service utilization

Prescription antidepressant use (linked to Report #4)

These measures provide insight into emotional wellbeing and mental health burden across demographic groups.

2. Depression Screening (PHQ‑9 Synthetic Scores)

Adults (20+ years):

Mean PHQ‑9 score: 6.1

Minimal symptoms (0–4): 42%

Mild depression (5–9): 32%

Moderate depression (10–14): 17%

Moderately severe/severe (15+): 9%

Adolescents (12–19 years):

Mean PHQ‑9 score: 7.4

Moderate or higher symptoms: 22%

Interpretation:  
Depressive symptoms are most common among adolescents and adults aged 20–39. Severe symptoms are less common but present across all demographic groups.

3. Anxiety Indicators

Anxiety symptoms were assessed using synthetic GAD‑7‑style values.

Adults (20+ years):

Mild anxiety: 29%

Moderate anxiety: 15%

Severe anxiety: 6%

Adolescents (12–19 years):

Moderate/severe anxiety: 18%

Narrative Analysis:  
Anxiety symptoms often co‑occur with depressive symptoms and are more prevalent among women and younger adults.

4. Psychological Distress (K6 Synthetic Scores)

Adults (20+ years):

Low distress (0–7): 63%

Moderate distress (8–12): 24%

Serious distress (13+): 13%

Interpretation:  
Serious psychological distress is strongly associated with low income, unemployment, and chronic disease burden.

5. Sleep Quality

Sleep quality is a major indicator of mental health.

Adults (20+ years):

Good sleep quality: 48%

Fair sleep quality: 34%

Poor sleep quality: 18%

Average Sleep Duration:

Adults: 6.7 hours/night

Adolescents: 7.4 hours/night

Narrative Analysis:  
Poor sleep quality is strongly associated with depressive symptoms, anxiety, and high screen time.

6. Stress Levels

Stress was assessed using synthetic self‑reported indicators.

Adults (20+ years):

Low stress: 36%

Moderate stress: 44%

High stress: 20%

Interpretation:  
High stress is most common among adults aged 30–49, reflecting work and family pressures.

7. Mental Health Service Utilization

Adults (20+ years):

Saw a mental health professional in past year: 22%

Took prescription antidepressants: 14%

Reported unmet mental health needs: 11%

Adolescents (12–19 years):

Received counseling: 18%

Reported unmet needs: 15%

Narrative Analysis:  
Unmet mental health needs are highest among uninsured and low‑income respondents.

8. Age‑Related Mental Health Patterns

Children (6–11 years):

Low depressive symptoms

Moderate anxiety variability

High emotional reactivity

Adolescents (12–19 years):

Highest depressive symptoms

High anxiety

High screen time and poor sleep

Adults (20–39 years):

High stress

Moderate depression and anxiety

Increasing antidepressant use

Adults (40–59 years):

Moderate depressive symptoms

High stress related to work and caregiving

Adults (60+ years):

Lower depression

Higher loneliness indicators

Lower mental health service utilization

9. Sex Differences

Women:

Higher depression and anxiety scores

Higher mental health service utilization

Higher antidepressant use

Men:

Higher serious psychological distress

Lower service utilization

Higher unmet mental health needs

10. Race & Ethnicity Differences

Non‑Hispanic Black:

Higher serious psychological distress

Lower mental health service utilization

Hispanic:

Higher unmet mental health needs

Moderate depression prevalence

Non‑Hispanic White:

Higher antidepressant use

Higher service utilization

Asian:

Lower reported symptoms

Lower service utilization (likely underreporting)

Interpretation:  
Mental health disparities reflect cultural, socioeconomic, and access‑related factors.

11. Socioeconomic Status & Mental Health

Low‑income respondents (IPR < 2.0):

Higher depression and anxiety

Higher serious psychological distress

Lower access to mental health services

Higher‑income respondents (IPR ≥ 2.0):

Higher service utilization

Lower distress

Higher antidepressant use

12. Key Findings

Depression and anxiety are most common among adolescents and younger adults.

Serious psychological distress affects 13% of adults.

Sleep quality is strongly linked to mental health symptoms.

Unmet mental health needs remain high, especially among low‑income respondents.

Sex and racial disparities persist across all mental health indicators.

13. Conclusion

This synthetic NHANES mental health report provides a realistic representation of U.S. emotional wellbeing patterns. The findings highlight depressive symptoms, anxiety trends, stress levels, and disparities in mental health service utilization. These patterns support downstream modeling, risk assessment, and NLP corpus development.
