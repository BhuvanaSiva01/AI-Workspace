Executive Summary

This synthetic NHANES physical examination report summarizes measured health indicators collected during standardized mobile examination center (MEC) visits. The dataset includes 10,000 respondents and reflects realistic U.S. patterns in anthropometry, blood pressure, body composition, and functional strength.

Key findings include high obesity prevalence, elevated blood pressure among middle‑aged adults, and age‑related declines in grip strength. These patterns mirror national trends in physical health and chronic disease risk.

1. Survey Description

NHANES physical examinations are conducted by trained health technicians using standardized protocols. This synthetic dataset mirrors NHANES methodology.

Measurements included:

Height

Weight

Body mass index (BMI)

Waist circumference

Blood pressure (three readings)

Grip strength

Resting pulse

Body composition (synthetic estimates)

These objective measures complement self‑reported health data.

Waist circumference is a key indicator of metabolic risk.

Mean Waist Circumference:

Narrative Analysis:  
High‑risk waist circumference is widespread and strongly associated with diabetes, hypertension, and high cholesterol.

4. Blood Pressure Measurements

Blood pressure was measured three times; the average of the second and third readings is used.

Mean Blood Pressure (Adults 20+ years):

Systolic: 126 mmHg

Diastolic: 78 mmHg

Blood Pressure Categories:

Normal (<120/80): 38%

Elevated (120–129/<80): 14%

Stage 1 Hypertension (130–139/80–89): 28%

Stage 2 Hypertension (≥140/≥90): 20%

Interpretation:  
Hypertension prevalence is high and increases sharply with age. Stage 2 hypertension is most common among adults aged 60+.

5. Grip Strength

Grip strength is a functional measure of muscular fitness.

Mean Grip Strength (Adults 20+ years):

Men: 41.2 kg

Women: 25.7 kg

Age‑Related Decline:

Ages 20–39: Strongest values

Ages 40–59: Moderate decline

Ages 60+: Significant decline

Narrative Analysis:  
Grip strength declines with age and correlates with mobility limitations and chronic disease burden.

Interpretation:  
Resting pulse varies by age, fitness level, and medication use. Higher pulse rates are observed among respondents with obesity and low physical activity.

8. Age‑Related Physical Examination Patterns

Children (0–19 years):

Lower BMI

Higher resting pulse

Lower blood pressure

Adults (20–39 years):

Increasing BMI

Moderate blood pressure

Strong grip strength

Adults (40–59 years):

Highest obesity prevalence

Rising hypertension

Declining grip strength

Adults (60+ years):

Highest blood pressure

Highest waist circumference

Lowest grip strength

9. Sex Differences

Men:

Higher height and weight

Higher grip strength

Higher systolic blood pressure

Women:

Higher body fat percentage

Higher obesity prevalence

Higher resting pulse

10. Key Findings

Obesity prevalence remains high across all demographic groups.

Hypertension affects nearly half of adults aged 40+.

Waist circumference indicates widespread metabolic risk.

Grip strength declines significantly with age.

Sex differences reflect biological and lifestyle factors.
